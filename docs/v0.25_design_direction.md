# Basements & Basilisks v0.25 — Design Direction

**Date:** 2026-11-23
**Status:** Agreed direction from a design session with Luke. Nothing here is implemented yet.
**Builds on:** `PROJECT_HANDOFF_v0.24.md` (§6 balance findings motivated most of this).

Items marked **(owner)** are Luke's decisions. Items marked **(proposed)** are suggestions from the session that still need his OK.

---

## 1. Why

The v0.24 tests (60 runs per bot style) found:
- A bot that drafts with no plan still wins 45% of runs, so the floor is too high.
- Building around shared tags barely pays off.
- Camp Rank and Scaling items cost more than they give back.

The fix is to make the choices *outside* combat decide runs, and to make individual heroes compelling rather than team-wide engines.

**Success test (proposed):** after the rework, the random-draft bot falls below 15% and a skilled bot clearly outperforms it, measured over 60+ runs per bot style.

---

## 2. Cuts for v0.25 (owner)

- Soft Lanes
- Interest
- Camp Rank (shop ranking up)
- House Rules, until there's a good solution. They are expected to return as team-wide keyword rules (§6).
- The branching map (DAG), replaced by the fixed Act structure in §9.

## 3. Design Pillars (owner)

1. **Individual heroes come first.** Each hero should be compelling on their own.
2. **Frontline and damage are the core.** Supports enable the frontline and damage dealers.
3. **Keyword interaction over engines.** Heroes have no built-in synergy; items and modifiers encourage playstyles and synergy.
4. **Stacking and scaling** encourage keeping heroes around. Higher-tier heroes should feel necessary enough that players want to pivot.
5. **Scaling is necessary,** and it comes from the decisions made outside combat.
6. **Meta decisions are key** to success and failure.
7. **Complexity grows naturally.** Heroes start doing one thing; the player's choices make them more multifaceted.
8. **More choices per hero, each simple and easy to read.** Color-code where it helps.

**Readability rule (owner, standing):** heroes and abilities stay concise, never a wall of text.
- Cards say *what happens* in one sentence per mechanic, e.g. "Inflict 1d4 poison."
- Hidden math and how status effects work live in **tooltips**, not on the card.
- **Universal rule: show only what the player needs to know.** Every stat, keyword, status and number leads with its result, the thing that matters to the player, first and biggest. Context (formulas, stack rules, how it's calculated) is fine print the player can look up in a tooltip or the Field Guide.
  - Example: hovering AC shows "Resists 38% physical damage"; the mitigation formula is fine print.
  - The same applies to spell resistance, attack speed, charge, CR, status effects and everything else.

Design rule (proposed): "scaling" means one hero growing stronger; "engine" means loops across several heroes. We want the first and less of the second.

## 4. Stats and Damage

**Ability score identities (owner):** each class has ability score identities, and every hero clearly shows which stats they scale with most.
- (proposed) One or two primary stats per class, e.g. Fighter STR/CON, Wizard INT, Rogue DEX.
- (proposed) Cards show "Scales with: STR", color-coded.
- (proposed) Items lean into one or two stats instead of the current "attribute parity" (+N to all six), which can't say who an item is for.

**Damage types (owner):** clear definitions for each damage type.
- (proposed) Three channels:
  - **Physical**, reduced by AC.
  - **Magic**, reduced by spell resistance.
  - **Radiant**, which ignores both.
- (proposed) Fire, poison and the like become flavor tags that items and keywords respond to, not extra damage rules. Today the engine labels damage on three overlapping axes (`kind`, `el`, `ty`).

**Dice-based damage model (owner):**
- Damage is built on dice for readability. Number of dice × die size is easy to read and feels impactful as it scales, in spite of roll variance.
- A flat **Damage Bonus** (usually ability-score based) adds consistency.
- Balance damage by: **(attack speed or cast rate × number of targets) × (NdX + Damage Bonus)**. Expected value per hit = N × (X+1)/2 + bonus.
- Status effects use the same metric. Card text reads "Inflict 1d4 poison" or a flat "Inflict 1 poison."

Proposed details:
- Three scaling sources:
  - **Tier adds dice** (1d8 → 2d8 → 3d8).
  - **Items and perks raise die size** (d6 → d8 → d10).
  - **The main ability score is the flat bonus.**
- When balancing against enemies, multiply by the fraction that gets through AC or spell resistance.
- The simulator prints expected damage per second for every hero, as a balance table.
- The flat bonus multiplies with speed and targets, so it applies to single-target attacks and cantrips; area abilities use dice only (as in D&D).
- **Poison:** deals its value per second, then drops by 1 (4 poison = 4+3+2+1 = 10 total). Stacks.
- **Burn:** deals its value per second for 3 seconds. A new burn replaces the old one instead of stacking.
- Cards show "2d6 + 3"; the tooltip shows the average (10). The Poison and Burn rules above live in tooltips, never on cards (see the readability rule in §3).

**WIS and CHA (owner):**
- Burn, poison and status resistance all become plain **magic damage / spell resistance**, which simplifies WIS.
- **CHA becomes a bonus to starting charge.**

Current code, for reference:
- WIS gives +1 spell resistance per point and a minimum d20 roll.
- CHA gives +8% strength on buffs and debuffs and +10% faster charge gain per point.
- Damage over time is only half-reduced by spell resistance.

Proposed details:
- Status effects (stun, charm and the like) are shortened by the same percentage that spell resistance cuts magic damage.
- Drop the WIS minimum d20 roll, or move it to a Cleric perk or an item, so WIS does one thing.
- CHA gives +10% starting charge per point, capped at 50%. It replaces the faster-charge effect rather than stacking with it.
- Burn and poison numbers likely need a bump, since spell resistance now applies in full.
- **Open:** buff and debuff strength loses its stat. Drop it, or move it to Bard/Paladin perks?

## 5. Hero Tiers (owner)

| Tier | What you get |
|---|---|
| 1 | The hero does one thing. |
| 2 | Changes the hero's playstyle. Unlocks their keyword, with effects to choose from. Unique to the hero and tied to their theme. |
| 3 | A generic class perk drawn from a deck unique to each class. Multiclassing adds the second class's deck to the pool. Options may be rerolled for gold (open). |
| 4 | Epic Boon (existing shared deck). |

- (proposed) The reroll cost rises each time (1, 2, 3…) and acts as a gold sink.
- **Open:** does a Tier 1 hero have a basic version of their keyword, or none until Tier 2? Without one, Tier 1 heroes can't use keyword items or team rules.

## 6. Keywords and Multiclassing (owner)

- **Multiclassing gives a basic keyword from another class,** so the hero benefits from that class's perks and keyword interactions.
- **Keywords have universal properties** plus hero-specific conditions that make them simpler to interact with. Examples:
  - Action Surge is always "Triggered."
  - Sneak Attack is always "Triggered" and works as a bonus attack on a cooldown.
- **Keywords have fun interactions not written on the card,** e.g. Smite + Sneak Attack for huge hits.
- **Team-wide rules** key off keywords, e.g. "When someone triggers Action Surge…". This is how House Rules come back.

Proposed details:
- Each keyword type already in the code (`CLASS_KW[].kind`) gets one fixed universal rule: Trigger, Mark, State, Resource, Token, Transformation, Presence.
- Hidden combos announce themselves: a big damage popup or a "Combo!" log line, plus a Field Guide entry once discovered.
- Limit each hero to one multiclass, so party identity doesn't blur.
- Bring team-wide rules back only after hero kits are solid, so balance tests stay readable.

## 7. Party (owner)

- Start with a party of **3**, plus **2 bench** slots.
- A **purchasable party expansion** (4th slot) is a reach goal in the shop.
- This replaces the locked "party always 4" rule.
- (proposed) Balance encounters around 3, so the 4th slot is a real power spike.
- (proposed) The bench lets you park a stacked hero while leveling a replacement, which softens the cost of pivoting.

---

## 8. Battle Map (owner)

- Heroes start on the **left**, enemies on the **right**.
- A narrower **6×8** map (read as 6 rows × 8 columns; confirm).
- **Fewer enemies, more intentionally designed encounters.**
- **One terrain element with real tactical impact per map.**

Proposed details:
- Deployment zone in columns 1–2, enemies in columns 7–8.
- Terrain ideas:
  - A one-at-a-time choke point.
  - High ground (+range or +AC).
  - Burning tiles.
  - Cover that blocks line of sight.
  - A chasm that splits the map into two routes.

## 9. Act Structure (owner)

An Act is **5 encounters and a boss**:

```
Shop → Fight 1 → Choice → Shop → Fight 2 → Choice → Shop → Fight 3 → Choice
→ Shop → Fight 4 → Choice → Shop → Fight 5 → Campfire → Shop → Boss → Rousing
```

(Assumes a shop before every fight, boss included; confirm.)

### Shop (owner)
- A regular shop before each fight.
- The shop is a window: the player can switch between the shop and the upcoming battle map to make informed decisions.
- (proposed) Merge deployment into the shop phase: scout, buy, place, then press Fight.

- **Hero offers drop to 3** per shop roll (currently `ECON.shopHeroes: 4`). (owner)
- (proposed) Choice cards or Rousing sometimes let the player pick a hero, since fewer offers make a specific hero harder to find.

### Choice after each fight (owner)
- Two options drawn from a deck.
- Examples: Multiclass Trainer, a class item, a free item, a bargain.
- Always appropriate to the power level of the current encounter.

Proposed details:
- Each card is tagged with the fights and Acts it can appear in, and its value scales by Act.
- The two options pull in different directions (power now vs. power later vs. risk).
- Example bargains:
  - "Next fight adds an elite; win for double gold."
  - "Skip this reward; next choice shows 3 options."

### Campfire after Fight 5 (owner)
A single universal choice to gather the party. Pick one:
- **Rank up one under-tiered hero:** Act 1 Tier 1→2, Act 2 Tier 2→3, Act 3 Tier 3→4.
- **Start the next fight (the boss) with a Ward.**
- **Another tier-up option for one hero** (read as an extra pick from a tier they already have; confirm).
- **Refresh the Piggy Bank.**

Proposed details:
- The Ward must be big enough to compete with rank-up, e.g. a shield worth 25–30% of max HP on every hero.
- Show a boss preview at the Campfire.

### Rousing after the boss (owner)
- A celebration with a large gold reward.
- A premium shop with unique, exclusive offers.

Proposed details:
- Offers: the party expansion slot, Legendary items, Epic Boon picks.
- One purchase per category.

## 10. Fight Length and Difficulty (owner)

- **Fights need to be longer.** This comes down to balancing enemy HP and defense.
- **Losing must be a real possibility,** and the player can tell what to improve for the next fight.
- **The 5 Act encounters are not steamrolls** unless the player is in a really good spot.

Measured in v0.24 (10 runs per bot style, 464 fights):
- Typical fight: **14.8 seconds**.
- 1 in 10 under 7.9 seconds; 1 in 10 over 33.2 seconds.
- Overtime starts at 28 seconds (`OT_START`).

Proposed targets and approach:
- A typical fight of about 35–45 seconds. Move overtime to about 60 seconds.
- Raise enemy HP more than AC or spell resistance. Mitigation is steep, so extra defense can quietly shut out one damage channel.
- Fewer, tougher enemies per map.
- In Fights 1–5, an average party wins with about 30–50% HP left; only a strong party wins with most of its HP.
- A post-fight summary shows:
  - damage dealt and taken per hero;
  - what killed the fallen heroes;
  - how close the fight was.

## 13. Suggested Implementation Order (proposed)

1. **Strip pass:** remove lanes, interest, Camp Rank, House Rules and the DAG map. Fix the test bot to draft with the game's real class keywords. Default balance tests to 60 runs.
2. **Foundations:** dice damage model and expected-damage table, stat identities, three damage channels, WIS/CHA changes, enemy HP and fight length, readable "Scales with" on cards.
3. **Structure:** party 3 + bench 2, the 6×8 map with terrain, the Act loop, the shop window, choice cards, Campfire, Rousing.
4. **Hero kits:** Tier 1 does one thing, Tier 2 playstyle choice, Tier 3 class decks, multiclass keywords.
5. **Scaling and economy:** stacking, bigger gold numbers, gold generators.
6. **Team-wide keyword rules** (House Rules return).
