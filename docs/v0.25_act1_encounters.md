# Basements & Basilisks v0.25: Act 1 Encounters (Lost Mine of Phandelver)

**Date:** 2026-11-24, second pass: every fight rebuilt from the enemy template (chassis × rank × affix)
**Status:** Proposed. Numbers come from a quick sim (below), not the game. Nothing is implemented. Retuned the same day after the cast-rate pass (`docs/v0.25_cast_rate_pass.md`): heroes now charge by job, so enemy HP came down about 10%.
**Builds on:** *B&B Design Templates: Heroes, Enemies, Items* (enemy chassis, ranks, affixes, encounter recipe), *B&B Balance Reference* (four knobs, tests, targets), the Design Bible's "What makes it fun", and the first pass of this doc (fight lessons, terrain, Luke's 24 Nov calls). Where they disagree, the templates win for numbers and this doc's earlier design calls win for what each fight teaches.

## The headline finding: Rank 1 was calibrated for four heroes

The templates' Rank 1 values came from a sim of the **Tavern four** against Klarg's Cave. Act 1 runs a **party of three** (Design Bible 0.25 structure; a 4th slot can be bought). Power scales with damage × durability, so three heroes have about (3/4)² ≈ 0.56× the power of four. In the sim:

- Tavern four vs Klarg's Cave at template values: 7% bare wins (the reference sim found 17%), so the sim is in the same range.
- The same fight with three heroes: 0% bare, 0% typical, 0% strong. A single Rank 1 Brute beats almost any lone T1 hero.
- Lowering only enemy damage (−1 to −3 flat per hit) didn't fix it: enemy HP was also about 5× what three T1 heroes can chew through in time.

**Proposed fix: Rank 1 sized for a party of three.** About 0.6–0.7× HP and 0.7× damage on every chassis, done with knob values only. Elites use **about 2× the chassis HP** (the template says about 2.5×). Bosses are hand-built at **5–7× chassis HP**. The encounter recipe and targeting rules are unchanged.

**Party size (decided, Luke, 24 Nov): Act 1 maxes at 3 heroes.** The 4th party slot is bought in the Rousing premium shop after the Act 1 boss. In the sim a fourth hero took the typical party to **100%** in every Act 1 fight, so keeping it out of Act 1 is what makes these numbers hold.

- **Elite:** chassis × about 2 HP (Klarg 1.6×) + one d20 ability, charged by job (Klarg from attacking, casters over time).
- **Boss:** chassis core × 5–7 HP, hand-built ability and turn at half HP. Half duration from control, half value from Advantage and Disadvantage.
- **Rank 0** (intro only): the chassis one step down, −1 attack die and about two-thirds HP.
- Affixes are as in the template. Act 1 uses Stunning (Ghouls), Warded (Glasstaff) and Pack (Cragmaw Wolves).

Targets (Balance Reference): bare usually loses, typical wins narrowly and often loses a hero, strong wins clearly. Bare drops and strong holds as the Act goes on. **Fight 2 can't separate bare from typical:** after Fight 1 the economy buys two heroes and almost no items, so both profiles field the same party. ### Fight 1: Goblin Ambush (Triboar Trail)

**Teaches:** how a fight reads. Your starter alone, melee foes only.

| Enemy | Chassis | HP | AC / SR | Attack |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Goblin ×2 | Swarmer, Rank 0 | 23 | 12 / 9 | 1d4+2 every 1.0s, melee |

Sim: every one of the 8 modelled starters wins 100%. Most take 9–13s and 1.6–2.4 casts. **Support starters run long:** Faelan 35s (7 casts), Bram 17s, Rosalind 15s. That's the existing open question about support starters, not an encounter problem.

### Fight 2: Klarg's Cave (Cragmaw Hideout)

**Teaches:** hold a line and pick a target. Design unchanged from Luke's 24 Nov calls.

| Enemy | Chassis | HP | AC / SR | Attack | Ability |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Klarg | Brute elite (1.6×) | 130 | 13 / 10 | Maul 4d6 every 1.6s (one heavy hit; the Brute's damage per second) | **Crushing Swing**, every 4th attack (~6.4s): 3d8+4 and Stun 1s on his target. Low: no stun. High: 4d8+4. |
| Ripper | Brute | 80 | 13 / 10 | Bite 2 × 2d6 every 1.6s | None |
| Goblin Archer | Shooter | 30 | 12 / 10 | 2d10+4 every 1.4s, range 4, nearest in range | None |

- **Terrain:** a narrow path along the edge lets a skirmisher reach the archer and turn it into a duel.
- **Tuning note:** the lever was Klarg's HP, not his swing. Changing the swing between 2d8 and 3d8+4 moved win rates about 5 points; Klarg at 2.5× vs 2× moved them about 30. This is why elites use about 2× for a party of three.

### Fight 3: Glasstaff's Hideout (Tresendar Manor)

**Teaches:** an enemy you have to reach while he picks at your line.

| Enemy | Chassis | HP | AC / SR | Attack | Ability or affix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Glasstaff | Shooter elite, **Warded** (+4 SR, −20% HP) | 48 | 12 / 14 | Arcane bolt 2d10+4 magic every 1.4s, range 4, nearest in range | **Magic Missile**, caster, about every 6s: 3 darts of 1d6+1 magic at his target. Low: 2 darts. High: also Wards himself 2d10+2 (his Staff of Defense, now a High result instead of a separate trigger). |
| Redbrand Ruffian ×2 | Bulwark, two-strike, −2 flat | 100 | 17 / 12 | 2 × 1d8 every 1.6s | Hold the bridge |

- **Terrain:** crevasse with one bridge. It blocks movement, not sight: range 4 reaches Glasstaff from the back, range 3 only from a forward square.
- **Ruffians hit a little under the Bulwark chassis** (9 a swing pair instead of 11): two walls that hold the bridge this long already set the pace, and their full damage made the fight harder than Thundertree.
- **Glass cannon by design:** 48 HP is low for an elite, but he's only reachable from range until the bridge opens. That's the bet: burst him from forward squares, or grind the Ruffians.

### Fight 4: Thundertree

**Teaches:** someone gets dragged in, so choose who. Prepares for King Grol.

| Enemy | Chassis | HP | AC / SR | Attack | Ability |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vine Blight | Controller elite | 120 | 13 / 13 | 2d8+1 magic every 2.0s; every 3rd hit roots 1.5s | **Grasping Vine**, once at the start: pulls the hero furthest from it onto a square beside it and roots them 1.5s. The target shows before the fight (vine icon). |
| Twig Blight ×4 | Swarmer | 35 | 12 / 9 | 2d4+1 every 1.0s | Guard the Vine Blight; swarm whoever is nearest (the pulled hero) |
| Needle Blight | Shooter, range 3, −4 flat | 30 | 12 / 10 | 2d10 every 1.4s | Keeps pressure on the heroes who weren't pulled |

- **Terrain:** ruined cottage around the Vine Blight with one doorway (allies need about 3–4s to get in).
- **Change from the first pass: 4 Twig Blights, not 3.** With 3, a bare party won most fights. A fourth twig (two Swarmer slots in all) with a weaker Needle Blight gives 16 / 53 / 100. The fight's danger belongs in the cottage, so the shot outside gives up 4 flat. Three twigs is a cliff (typical 90%).
- **The lesson works in the sim.** The strong bot puts its sturdiest hero in the rearmost square as bait. That one placement choice is most of the strong–typical gap.

### Fight 5: Forge of Spells (Wave Echo Cave)

**Teaches:** spread out, or bunch up on the Forge and pay for it. Prepares for Venomfang's breath.

| Enemy | Chassis | HP | AC / SR | Attack | Ability or affix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flameskull | Artillery elite | 120 | 12 / 16 | 4d6 magic every 2.5s to the target and adjacent heroes, range 4 | **Fireball**, caster, about every 11s: 6d6 to the densest clump. Low 4d6, High 8d6. |
| Lesser Flameskull | Artillery | 60 | 12 / 16 | As above | None |
| Ghoul ×2 | Brute, **Stunning** (−15% HP) | 68 | 13 / 10 | 2 × 2d6 every 1.6s | Every 3rd hit on one hero stuns 0.5s |

- **Change from the first pass: one elite Flameskull and one regular, not two elites.** Two elites (even with a 4d6 Fireball) were clearly too hard for typical parties. The regular one keeps the back-line AoE without a second Fireball.
- Fireball moved to a slower, bigger cast (6d6 every ~11s rather than 3d6+2 every ~8s), so it's a moment the player watches for. Area abilities use dice only, per the Balance Reference.
- **Physical and Radiant do better** here (Artillery has SR 16). One high resistance per regular, so magic teams still win, just slower.

### Boss: Nezznar, the Black Spider

**Tests:** control. Can your team fight while held in place?

| | |
|---|---|
| Chassis | Controller core × 5 |
| HP, AC / SR | 310, 14 / 16 |
| Attack | 2d8+6 magic every 2.0s, range 4 |
| Ability | **Web**, caster, about every 7s: roots the 2 nearest heroes 2s (High 3s), 2d6+2 each |
| Turn at half HP | **Darkness:** for 6s, he and anyone next to him can't be targeted from range |
| Adds | **3 Giant Spiders** (Brute, 80 HP, 2 × 2d6 every 1.6s). No poison: poison is Venomfang's twist. |

- **Change: 3 spiders, not 2.** With 2, a bare party won most fights; Nezznar alone isn't a threat to three heroes. Nezznar himself drops to 310 HP to make room for the third spider.
- **Narrow strong–typical gap** (77% vs 65%). The sim's strong bot doesn't play the ranged-first, melee-second plan, so the real gap should be bigger. Check this fight first on the real engine.

### Boss: King Grol (Cragmaw Castle)

**Tests:** the dive. Hold the gate while Grol charges over your line.

| | |
|---|---|
| Chassis | Brute core × 7 |
| HP, AC / SR | 570, 16 / 12 |
| Attack | Maul 4d6 every 1.6s; 30% faster after his turn at half HP |
| Ability | **Brutal Charge**, caster, about every 10s: charges the rearmost hero for 4d8+4 and Stun 1s (Low 3d8+4, no stun; High 1.5s), then stays on them |
| Adds | **2 Cragmaw Wolves** (Swarmer, **Pack**: adjacent allies attack 15% faster; 28 HP, 2d4+1 every 1.0s) |

- **Change: the Goblin Archer is cut.** With it, even the strong bot won under half: Grol's charge plus an archer on a stunned back line was two dive threats in one fight. Without it the fight lands in range (now 6 / 57 / 74).
- **Wolves are Swarmers with Pack, not Divers.** The template maps Wolf to Diver, but a leap at fight start would jump the gate and stack a third dive on Grol's. Diver wolves fit better in a later Act.
- The strong bot puts its sturdiest hero in the rearmost square, as at Thundertree. In the sim that helps less than it should: the bot doesn't move a peeler back.

## What the sim says beyond win rates

- **Cast-off check (centrality only; casts don't need to swing fights, Luke, 24 Nov).** Per hero, across Fights 2–5, turning one ability off drops win rates: Faelan 54% → 5%, Arthur 73% → 17%, Bram 49% → 15%, Zark 64% → 22%, Wallaby 52% → 25%, Lorcan 53% → 29%, Rosalind 37% → 25%, Sir Dane 35% → 29%. Faelan carries her parties too much, a support-centrality risk for the identity pass.
- **The tank casts about 2.2–4.2 times a fight** in regular fights and 3.8–5.6 at bosses: at or above the target of at least 2.
- **Too many heroes die.** Typical parties lose a hero in 63–99% of their wins (99% at Thundertree, where the pulled hero usually falls); the target is a third to half. With Last Stand earned (only Zark has it among the modelled heroes), one death is the norm. Worth watching, not fixing yet: the real engine has positioning and heal targeting that this sim doesn't.
- **HP lost in wins runs high,** about 61–81% for typical parties (target: 30–50% left, so 50–70% lost). Slightly hot, worst at the Forge.
- **A summed budget doesn't predict difficulty.** By effective HP × damage per second, Thundertree is the cheapest regular fight, yet it beats bare parties more than Klarg's Cave. The lesson elements (the pull, the clump, the bridge) do much of the work. The recipe's "budget above a bare party" is a floor, not a dial.
- **Fight lengths:** regular fights 22–35s (target 25–35s); bosses 41–55s (guardrail up to 45s). Bosses could trade some HP for damage.

## How the sim works (and its limits)

Python, in `tools/act1-sim/` (`act1sim.py` engine and heroes, `castpass.py` job charge and resized abilities, `encounters.py` fight rules, `final.py` these drafts, `tune.py` the table, `metrics.py` per-hero casts). Run `python3 final.py`.

- Rules: linear resistance R/(R+20), min 1 damage, crits double dice, Radiant ignores AC and SR, control reads SR at half, Last Stand only for Half-Orcs, d20 bands for every ability.
- Heroes: 8 T1 kits from `claude/proto-0.25/heroes.js` (in the claude.ai Project) (Arthur, Bram, Faelan, Wallaby, Sir Dane, Lorcan, Rosalind, Zark), prototype HP formula. At bosses one hero is T2 (Campfire). Charge follows the job rule with the cast-rate pass values.
- Parties: random 3-hero drafts, 300 per profile. **Bare:** no items, front/back by range. **Typical:** always has a tank; Common-item equivalents from the economy (0.3 / 0.9 / 2.0 / 3.4 / 4.6). **Strong:** typical +1 item, plus the fight's key play (archer first; forward ranged at Glasstaff; sturdiest hero as Thundertree bait and Grol's rear; spread at Forge and Venomfang).
- Fixed seeds: the same 300 drafts per profile in every fight, so fights compare directly.
- **Not modelled:** pathing and movement, the doorway and bridge beyond timing rules, Ward replacement rules, Pack's attack speed, the Controller's root on its 3rd hit, push effects. Numbers are directions for the real engine, not final values.

## Open

- **Act 2 is sized for two paths (Luke, 24 Nov):** four heroes, or three heroes with more tier-ups, items and perks. Exchange rate from the sim, measured before the cast-rate pass (boss stand-ins scaled up so a four-hero party wins about 51%): a typical Act 1-end party of three (one T2, 4.6 Common-equivalents each) wins 8–11%. Matching the fourth hero took **all three heroes at T2 and about 3 more items each** (51–53%). So the fourth hero ≈ 2 tier-ups + about 4–5 items for the party.
- **Proposed 4th slot price: about 55 gold** (plus the hero, 15). The other path costs about 2 × 25 (tier-ups) + 4.4 × 5 (Commons) ≈ 72 gold, so slot plus hero ≈ 70 keeps the two paths even. That's most of the 75-gold Rousing reward, which makes the slot the Rousing shop's headline choice. Likely an upper bound: the sim's T2 only adds dice and HP, without Class Features or perks, so it undervalues the three-hero path. Re-test once Act 2 enemies exist.
- **Elite multiplier:** about 2× for a party of three, against the template's "about 2.5×". Update the template if Luke agrees.
- Re-run on the real engine (prototype or repo) with jobs, pathing and the linear curve, starting with Nezznar and King Grol, where the sim's strong bot is weakest.
- Hero deaths in wins run above target; check after the real-engine run.
- Kaska rework and support starters' long Fight 1 are unchanged from the first pass.
